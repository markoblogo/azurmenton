# A Beach Day in Menton: What It Costs and What to Bring

There are two very different ways to spend a beach day in Menton.

The first involves a towel, some sunscreen, a bottle of water and a comfortable spot on a public beach.

The second involves a reserved sunbed, a parasol, drinks and perhaps lunch at a private beach restaurant.

Both can be enjoyable.

But they have very different costs, and paying more doesn't necessarily mean that you'll enjoy the sea more.

Menton has several public beaches, including the popular Plage des Sablettes beneath the colourful old town, Plage du Marché near the centre and Plage Rondelli towards Garavan. Step-free access needs a separate, beach-specific check.

There are also private beach establishments that rent sunbeds and provide additional services during their operating season.

The choice depends on how much time you want to spend on the beach, whether you need shade, what you're prepared to carry and how much comfort matters to you.

This guide compares both approaches using published prices, explains what is and isn't normally included, and provides a practical packing list.

The aim is simple: choose the beach day you'll actually enjoy, then know what it is likely to cost before you leave your accommodation.

## 1. Public beach or private beach club?

The basic distinction is straightforward.

### A public beach

You bring your own equipment and choose an available place in the public beach area.

You can normally enjoy the beach without paying admission.

You provide your own towel, sun protection and any extra comfort items you need.

Public beach areas are subject to local rules, weather conditions and any temporary restrictions.

### A private beach establishment

You pay to rent equipment or use a managed beach area.

Depending on the operator, this may include a sunbed, parasol or other services.

Some establishments also operate restaurants and bars.

Prices and services vary by season, date and location.

### What paying doesn't guarantee

A rented sunbed doesn't guarantee calm water, suitable swimming conditions, lifeguard coverage or freedom from crowds.

A private establishment may provide additional comfort, but sea conditions and safety precautions still matter.

Private-beach tariffs are typically charged per person or sunbed, not per group. Confirm the exact charging basis before booking.

Also ask whether the rate includes a parasol, whether towels are supplied and whether front-row places cost more.

A restaurant reservation doesn't necessarily include sunbed use, and a sunbed booking doesn't necessarily include food or drinks.

### Why prices may change

Private beaches operate seasonally.

Tariffs may vary during July and August, weekends, public holidays and high-demand periods.

Online listings may show an earlier season's prices.

Always confirm the total before booking, especially if travelling during the following summer.

## 6. Which public beach should you choose in Menton?

Menton has several beaches with different surroundings and practical advantages.

### Plage des Sablettes: the classic Menton view

Best for: A scenic beach day, old-town views, nearby restaurants and a longer relaxed stay.

Sablettes is one of Menton's most recognisable beaches.

It lies below the historic centre, with colourful buildings and the Saint-Michel bell tower above the waterfront.

The beach area includes public sections and nearby private beach establishments.

The redesigned esplanade offers promenades, public seating and other amenities.

### Why choose it?

The setting is particularly attractive.

There are places nearby to eat, stroll or take a break from the beach.

And you don't have to spend the entire day lying on a towel to enjoy the area.

### What to check

Confirm the boundaries of the public beach and any managed concessions.

Check swimming conditions and the current availability of facilities.

The beach is popular, especially during peak summer periods, so don't assume you'll find an uncrowded space at any time.

### Plage du Marché: convenient for central Menton

Best for: Visitors staying near the town centre who want a short swim without complex logistics.

Plage du Marché is close to the central waterfront and covered market.

Its location makes it practical for a spontaneous visit.

You can bring a towel, spend some time beside the sea and return to your accommodation without turning the trip into a full-day excursion.

### Why choose it?

Convenience.

You may already be within walking distance.

It can be easier to combine with a morning shopping trip or lunch in town.

### What to check

Beach surfaces, local facilities, sea conditions and any seasonal restrictions.

Don't assume every section offers the same accessibility or comfort.

### Plage Rondelli: an eastern Menton alternative

Best for: Visitors staying towards Garavan or looking for a beach away from the central promenade.

Rondelli lies in the eastern part of Menton, closer to the Italian border.

Its location can make it a convenient alternative depending on where you're staying.

### Why choose it?

You can combine a beach visit with time in the Garavan neighbourhood.

It's particularly useful if reaching Sablettes or the central beaches would involve unnecessary travel.

### What to check

Current access, sea conditions, public facilities and the precise location of the public beach area.

Don't assume that a beach is quieter merely because it lies farther from the centre.

## 7. What do you actually get at a private beach?

The published sunbed price is only the beginning of the comparison.

Before reserving, confirm what the establishment provides.

### Sunbed

Does the booking include a full-size lounger with a cushion?

Is the price per person, per lounger or per package?

### Parasol

Is shade included?

Does the parasol serve one lounger or two?

Can it be adjusted as the sun moves?

### Towel

Are towels provided, rented separately or not supplied?

### Showers and changing facilities

Are there showers and changing areas?

Are they included in the sunbed price?

### Restaurant and drinks

Can you order food at your lounger, or do you need a restaurant table?

Is there a minimum spending requirement?

### Booking and cancellation

What happens if the weather changes?

Can you cancel?

Is there a refund if the sea is too rough for swimming?

### Half-day arrangements

Some establishments publish morning or afternoon rates, but others only offer a full-day booking.

Check the actual time at which half-day access begins or ends.

A €25 half-day offer isn't automatically better value than a €35 full-day offer if you intend to stay for several hours.

## 8. What to pack for a public beach

A little preparation makes a public beach considerably more comfortable.

### Essentials

- Swimsuit and towel.

- Sunscreen suited to your needs.

- Drinking water.

- Hat or other sun protection.

- Sunglasses.

- Appropriate footwear, particularly for pebbly surfaces.

- A bag to keep your belongings together.

- Phone, keys and a payment method.

- Any medication or personal essentials you need.

### Useful extras

A beach mat or lightweight cushion can make sitting on stones or pebbles more comfortable.

A reusable water bottle can reduce the need to buy drinks.

A small insulated bag is useful if you are bringing food that requires temperature control.

A book or headphones can make a long beach afternoon more enjoyable.

And a light change of clothing may be helpful if you plan to stop at a café afterwards.

### What to leave behind

Avoid bringing unnecessary valuables.

Don't leave a passport, expensive camera or other important items unattended while swimming.

And don't bring more equipment than you can comfortably carry back.

For a short visit from a central apartment, a towel and a few essentials may be enough.

## 9. What to bring to a private beach

Even if you're renting a sunbed, you still need to prepare.

Bring your sunscreen, swimwear, personal medication and necessary valuables.

Confirm whether a towel is supplied.

Check whether outside food and drinks are allowed.

And carry a payment method accepted by the establishment.

There is usually no reason to bring your own beach umbrella if your booking includes one.

The main advantage of the paid format is that it can reduce the amount of equipment you carry.

But it won't eliminate every practical consideration.

## 10. Sea conditions and swimming safety

A beautiful sunny day doesn't necessarily mean calm or safe water.

Before swimming, check local conditions, warnings and any lifeguard arrangements.

### Don't assume every beach has year-round supervision

Supervised swimming is often seasonal.

The presence of a lifeguard station doesn't mean it is staffed at the time of your visit.

### Pay attention to flags and notices

Beach flags and official signs communicate current conditions and restrictions.

Follow local instructions.

### Pebbles, rocks and entries into the water

Some Menton beaches have pebbly or mixed surfaces.

Underwater footing and water depth can change.

Suitable footwear may help with comfort, but it does not remove all risks.

### Swimming with children

Keep children appropriately supervised near and in the water.

A rented lounger, nearby restaurant or busy beach is not a substitute for active supervision.

### Swimming alone

Assess your abilities, the current water conditions and the availability of assistance.

Avoid going beyond your limits or assuming that other beachgoers will notice immediately if you need help.

### In an emergency

Call 112 for emergency assistance in France.

Give your location as precisely as possible, including the beach name and nearby access point.

## 11. Shade matters more than you might expect

The Mediterranean sun can be intense, particularly in summer.

A beach umbrella can therefore be a practical necessity for visitors who plan to stay for several hours.

This is one of the strongest arguments for a paid beach setup when you don't have your own suitable shade equipment.

### Public beach shade

Don't assume you'll find natural shade on a public beach.

Buildings, walls and trees may shade certain sections at particular hours, but the position of the sun changes.

A shaded bench on the esplanade isn't the same as a shaded place beside the water.

### Private beach shade

Confirm that a parasol is included and can provide adequate shade for your chosen place.

A front-row sunbed may offer a more open view but also greater exposure.

### A practical alternative

Instead of staying on the beach through the hottest part of the day, organise a shorter morning visit and return to your accommodation for lunch.

This can be particularly convenient if you're staying in a central Menton apartment.

## 12. Eating during your beach day

Food choices can significantly change the total cost.

### Cheapest practical option

Prepare food in your apartment, pack it appropriately and bring sufficient water.

This works best when you're close to the beach and don't need to transport perishable food for too long.

### Middle option

Buy sandwiches or takeaway food from a suitable shop.

Check the shop's actual opening hours, especially on Sundays and public holidays.

### Restaurant lunch

A proper lunch can be part of the pleasure of the day.

But a restaurant visit may take longer than expected, particularly during peak summer hours.

If you're using a private beach, check whether the beach booking and restaurant reservation are separate.

### Food allergies

If anyone in your group has a food allergy, don't assume that a beach restaurant can accommodate it.

Ask about ingredients, preparation and cross-contact, and have a suitable alternative where necessary.

Do not rely on a general menu description as confirmation that a meal is suitable. Discuss your specific requirements directly with the restaurant.

## 13. How to organise a half-day beach visit

You don't need to spend six hours on the sand to enjoy the Mediterranean.

### Morning plan

Arrive relatively early.

Choose your preferred public beach or reserved lounger.

Swim or relax according to the conditions.

Have a coffee or light snack.

Return to your apartment for lunch.

Suggested beach time: Two to three hours.

This works well for travellers who want to enjoy the sea without committing the entire day.

### Afternoon plan

Have lunch before going to the beach.

Arrive later in the day.

Spend an hour or two swimming, reading or relaxing.

Return before dinner.

This can be a good alternative when your morning is occupied by shopping or a guided visit.

## 14. How to organise a full beach day

For a longer day, comfort and facilities become more important.

### Example schedule

9:30 a.m. Arrive and settle in.

10:00 a.m. Enjoy the water if conditions are suitable.

11:30 a.m. Take a break in the shade.

12:30 p.m. Lunch.

2:00 p.m. Rest, read or enjoy the surrounding promenade.

4:00 p.m. Decide whether to swim again or leave.

The times are flexible suggestions, not a scheduled activity.

A full day doesn't mean spending every minute in direct sun or in the water.

If the weather changes or you become uncomfortable, leave earlier.

## 15. Beach days with babies and toddlers

For families with very young children, the most important questions may have little to do with the view.

Where will the child nap?

Is there enough shade?

Can you prepare or buy suitable food?

How quickly can you return to the apartment?

Are toilets and changing facilities available?

### Public beach

The advantage is flexibility.

You can leave after twenty minutes without feeling that you've wasted an expensive reservation.

The disadvantage is that you'll usually need to provide more of your own equipment.

### Private beach

A lounger and parasol may make a longer visit more comfortable.

But you still need to confirm the establishment's policies, space and facilities for children.

A beach club is not automatically designed for infants or strollers.

For children under three, a short morning beach visit followed by lunch and a nap in the apartment may be more manageable than a full-day booking.

Our family guide offers more ideas for balancing beach time with other activities in Menton.

## 16. Accessibility: don't confuse beach access with sea access

A seaside promenade may be accessible even when reaching the water is difficult.

The distinction matters.

### Questions to check

Can you reach the promenade without steps?

Is there a suitable path from the promenade to the beach?

Does the beach have adapted equipment?

Are accessible toilets available?

Is assistance offered during the relevant season?

Are there suitable places to sit without descending onto the sand or pebbles?

These questions need current, beach-specific answers.

### Les Sablettes

The redesigned esplanade includes documented public seating, drinking fountains and toilets. A facility listed on a website is not confirmation that it is operating when you arrive.

However, that doesn't establish that every part of the beach or entry into the sea is independently accessible.

Visitors with specific mobility needs should verify the relevant facilities directly.

Menton's Handiplage service has its own seasonal arrangements: neither assistance nor adapted bathing equipment should be assumed available outside the announced season. Confirm the next season directly before planning around it. For routes with reduced walking and fewer stairs, see Menton Without Stairs.

## 17. A simple decision: which beach format is right for you?

| Your priority | More practical starting point |
| --- | --- |
| Spend as little as possible | Public beach |
| Quick swim for an hour | Public beach |
| Full day reading comfortably | Private lounger may be worthwhile |
| Guaranteed equipment reservation | Booked private beach, subject to confirmation |
| Maximum flexibility to leave early | Public beach |
| Avoid carrying an umbrella and lounger | Private beach |
| Family with a toddler and uncertain nap schedule | Short public beach outing |
| Special occasion with lunch service | Private beach and restaurant booking |
| Need verified accessible facilities | Choose by confirmed access details, not public/private status |

The important point is that the public and private formats solve different problems.

A public beach maximises freedom and reduces spending.

A private beach offers equipment and services that may make a longer stay more comfortable.

Neither is universally better.

## 18. Frequently asked questions

### Are public beaches in Menton free?

Public beach areas are generally free to access. Private beach establishments charge for loungers and other services.

### How much is a sunbed in Menton?

The reference sources show €30–42 for a full day, but two tourist-office listings date from August 2025. This is not a confirmed current or 2027 price range. Ask for a quotation for your actual date.

### Can I reserve a sunbed for half a day?

Some establishments publish half-day offers, while others do not. Check the operator's booking conditions.

### Is a parasol included?

It depends on the establishment and offer. Some published rates include a parasol; others require clarification.

### Which beach has the famous old-town view?

Plage des Sablettes, below Menton's colourful historic centre.

### Which beach is convenient for central Menton?

Plage du Marché can be a practical choice for visitors staying near the town centre.

### Are the beaches sandy?

Menton has beaches with varying mixtures of sand, pebbles and other coastal surfaces. Check the specific beach rather than expecting soft sand everywhere.

### Do private beaches have lifeguards?

Supervision arrangements vary by establishment and season. Do not assume paid access guarantees supervised swimming.

### Can I bring my own food?

On public beaches, you can generally bring a picnic subject to local rules. Private establishments may restrict outside food and drinks.

### Can I use a private beach restaurant without renting a sunbed?

Policies vary. Contact the restaurant directly.

### Is a private beach worth the cost?

It may be if you're spending a full day and value the comfort, shade and services. For a short swim, a public beach is usually the more economical option.

### Do I need to book in advance?

For private loungers during busy periods, advance booking is sensible. Public beach access doesn't normally require a reservation.

### Can I visit the beach in winter?

Public waterfronts and beaches remain part of Menton's landscape outside the summer season, but weather, water conditions and private-beach services vary considerably.

### What should I bring?

At minimum, a towel, appropriate sun protection, drinking water, suitable footwear, your essentials and any medication you need.

## Final thoughts: the best beach day is the one that suits you

A beach day in Menton doesn't need to be expensive.

You can bring a towel, find a suitable place on a public beach and enjoy the same Mediterranean scenery as visitors paying for a private sunbed.

Or you can book a lounger, settle beneath a parasol and make the beach your main destination for the day.

Both approaches have their advantages.

The sensible choice depends on how long you want to stay, what equipment you already have, how much you're willing to carry and what kind of comfort you expect.

Check the conditions.

Compare the full price, not just the advertised sunbed rate.

Bring what you need.

And leave room to change your plans if the weather, sea or your mood changes.

After all, the Mediterranean is the main attraction.

And the view doesn't come with a compulsory sunbed reservation.
