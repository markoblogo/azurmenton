# February on the French Riviera: How to Combine Menton's Lemon Festival and Nice Carnival

February is an unusual time to visit the French Riviera.

The sea is too cold for most swimmers, the days are shorter than in summer and some beach businesses are closed.

Yet for a few weeks, two of the region's most spectacular festivals transform the coastline.

In Menton, enormous sculptures made from citrus fruit fill the gardens, while elaborate floats parade along the seafront during the famous Fête du Citron.

In Nice, giant carnival figures, extravagant costumes, music and flower-covered floats take over the city centre.

Both festivals take place in February, and their dates overlap.

That makes it possible to experience two major Riviera celebrations during a single holiday without changing accommodation.

The secret is choosing your dates carefully.

Four nights can be enough for both events. Five offers a more comfortable balance. Six allows time to explore the Riviera between the festivities.

This guide is about putting those days together, rather than explaining every individual parade or ticket category. For detailed venue information, use our existing Lemon Festival guide and the two festival event pages.

## 2. Which length of stay should you choose?

| Stay | Best for | Recommended approach |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 4 nights | Visitors with limited holiday time | One major event in each city, one quieter day |
| 5 nights | Most first-time visitors | Two Menton experiences, one Nice event, recovery time |
| 6 nights | Families and slow travellers | Two festival cities, extra rest, one regional excursion |

Four nights is a practical minimum for enjoying both destinations without spending every day travelling.

Five nights provides a better balance.

Six nights is useful if you have younger children, want to attend both day and evening events, or prefer a more relaxed pace.

## 3. Our recommended five-night itinerary: 18–23 February 2027

This is our preferred planning window because it includes two confirmed major Menton events.

### Thursday, 18 February: arrive in Menton and see the illuminated parade

Arrive during the day and settle into your accommodation.

Allow enough time to unpack, buy basic groceries and familiarise yourself with the surrounding streets.

Menton's night-time Corso des Fruits d'Or is scheduled for 9 p.m. on Promenade du Soleil.

The official organiser recommends that visitors coming to Menton on parade Thursdays arrive before 6 p.m. because of road closures and congestion.

If you're already staying centrally, you have a considerable advantage.

Have an early dinner, walk to your booked entrance and enjoy the illuminated floats.

The published programme concludes with a pyrotechnic display around 10:30 p.m.

Afterwards, return to your accommodation without relying on a late-night train.

Why this works: The most logistically difficult Menton event happens near your base, rather than at the end of a long regional excursion.

### Friday, 19 February: recover and enjoy the citrus gardens

After the previous night's festivities, keep the morning relaxed.

Have breakfast, visit the covered market or walk along the Mediterranean.

During the day, explore Menton's citrus sculpture exhibition at Jardins Biovès.

The evening Jardins de Lumières programme is also scheduled for 19 February.

You can choose between a daytime exhibition visit and an illuminated evening experience depending on energy, ticket availability and the weather.

Don't feel obliged to attend both.

If you have young children or prefer a quieter day, a morning garden visit followed by an afternoon at the apartment may be enough.

### Saturday, 20 February: Nice Carnival day

Take the regional train to Nice after breakfast.

Spend the late morning exploring the city centre, Cours Saleya or the old town.

This is the day reserved for a major Nice Carnival experience.

The official programme lists a flower parade on 20 February at 14:30–16:00 and an illuminated corso at 20:30–22:00. For a simpler return and a family outing, prefer the afternoon session; availability still needs checking.

If both an afternoon and evening performance are available, don't automatically book both.

An entire day among festival crowds can be exhausting, especially for children.

Choose the event you care about most, then arrange lunch or dinner around it.

The date and times above are published sessions, checked on 4 November 2026. Our choice of outing is an editorial recommendation, not a ticket reservation or assurance against programme changes.

Return to Menton using a verified train or another agreed transport arrangement.

### Sunday, 21 February: Menton's daytime citrus parade

The main Menton daytime Corso is confirmed for 2:30 p.m. on Sunday, 21 February.

The organiser recommends that visitors arriving from outside Menton reach the town before 11 a.m.

If you're already staying locally, you can enjoy a quiet morning without travelling through the festival traffic.

Have breakfast, rest and eat an early lunch.

Then walk to the entrance named on your ticket. The organiser currently marks the 21 February grandstands as full: do not build this day around an assumed seat. If standing is unsuitable, check another official session/category before committing.

Allow time for security checks and crowd management.

The daytime event is an opportunity to see the extraordinary citrus-covered floats in natural light.

Afterwards, keep the evening free.

You will already have experienced several major events over four days.

### Monday, 22 February: a proper day off

Don't book another parade simply because you have an open day.

Use it to enjoy Menton without a programme.

Walk along the seafront, explore the lower old town, have lunch at a restaurant or take a quiet regional excursion if you feel like travelling.

A visit to Monaco could work, but it's entirely optional.

You may discover that a slow day is exactly what you need after the festival crowds.

### Tuesday, 23 February: departure

Have breakfast, pack and enjoy a final short walk if your departure time allows.

If you're staying an additional night, the illuminated citrus gardens also have a confirmed programme on Tuesday, 23 February.

Don't squeeze in a paid attraction if it creates pressure around luggage collection or a train connection.

## 6. A separate itinerary for families with children

The ideal festival trip with children isn't necessarily the one with the most parades.

Crowds, loud music, security barriers, waiting times and winter evening temperatures can become tiring.

For younger children, we'd recommend a five- or six-night stay with fewer late events.

### Family-friendly five-night template: 18–23 February

Day 1, Thursday 18: arrive and settle in; skip the 21:00 Menton parade if too late for the children.

Day 2, Friday 19: see the citrus exhibition by daylight, then rest.

Day 3, Saturday 20: choose Nice's published 14:30 flower parade if suitable tickets are available; return with a checked daytime/evening connection.

Day 4, Sunday 21: consider Menton's 14:30 parade only if the ticket category suits everyone. Grandstands are marked full at this review; standing may be unsuitable for small children. It is fine to choose just one city's parade instead of two consecutive crowd days.

Day 5, Monday 22: a proper local rest day, playground or time at the apartment.

Day 6, Tuesday 23: depart.

This family version uses the same five nights as the main example but removes the late-night commitments. Published sessions do not guarantee tickets, accessibility or an appropriate experience for every age.

### Babies and toddlers

For children under three, the citrus exhibition may be more practical than a late evening parade.

Choose short outings with easy access back to the accommodation.

A pushchair can be useful for travelling around Menton, but crowd barriers, standing areas and security rules may create complications.

Check the organiser's specific arrangements before attending.

### School-age children

Older children may particularly enjoy a flower battle in Nice, where the decorated floats are a major attraction.

Choose seats or viewing areas that suit your family's needs.

Bring warm layers, snacks where permitted and allow time for toilet breaks.

### Teenagers

Teenagers may enjoy the more energetic parades, photography and the spectacular evening lighting.

Give them some involvement in choosing the events, but establish meeting points and return arrangements beforehand.

### A useful reminder

The Fête du Citron requires an admission ticket even for children who qualify for free entry.

For Nice Carnival, check the current age-based ticket rules separately.

Don't assume one festival's child policy applies to the other.

## 7. How to buy tickets without making expensive mistakes

Book the two festivals separately.

They are organised by different authorities, with different ticketing systems and admission rules.

### Menton Lemon Festival

Use the official Fête du Citron website:

[Menton Lemon Festival official website](https://www.fete-du-citron.com/)

The main ticket categories include daytime parades, night-time parades, citrus exhibitions and illuminated gardens. Daytime and illuminated garden visits are distinct products unless an explicit package says otherwise.

Some areas offer seated grandstands while others are standing sections.

Tickets for better grandstand positions can sell out, so book early if seating is important.

### Nice Carnival

Use the official Carnival website:

[Nice Carnival official website](https://www.nicecarnaval.com/en/)

Check the date, event type and whether you're buying a grandstand seat or standing admission.

A Flower Parade isn't the same performance as an illuminated Carnival Corso.

If your main interest is seeing elaborately decorated floral floats, book the correct event rather than a generic Carnival ticket.

### What should you reserve first?

For this type of multi-day trip, use the following order:

- Identify a confirmed Menton parade date.

- Match it with a confirmed Nice Carnival event.

- Choose a four-, five- or six-night travel window.

- Check accommodation availability and total price.

- Reserve the festival tickets and accommodation in a coordinated way, taking cancellation conditions into account.

- Organise transport and any restaurant reservations.

Published sessions are now available, but recheck date, time, ticket category and return transport before non-refundable commitments.

## 8. Menton or Nice: where should you stay?

Both cities can work as a base.

But they offer different advantages.

### Why choose Menton?

Menton is the natural choice if the Lemon Festival is central to your trip and you want a quieter place between events.

You can walk to the citrus sculptures and main parade area from many central locations.

That makes the evening parades particularly convenient.

You'll also avoid having to take a late train back from Menton after the night-time Corso.

On recovery days, the town offers a more relaxed atmosphere than central Nice during Carnival.

An apartment with a kitchen provides additional flexibility for breakfasts, early dinners and time indoors.

### Why choose Nice?

Nice is the better base if Carnival nightlife and repeated evening events are the main purpose of your visit.

Staying centrally can eliminate late-night transport after an illuminated parade.

You'll also have more urban entertainment, restaurants and cultural options outside the festival.

The trade-off is that attending a late Menton Corso requires a journey back to Nice.

### Our recommendation

For one major Nice event and two or more Menton festival experiences, choose Menton.

For several Nice evening performances and only one Menton day trip, Nice may be more practical.

Don't choose your base solely by comparing distance on a map.

Choose it according to where you'll finish your latest evenings.

## 9. Transport between Menton and Nice during festival season

Regional TER trains connect Menton and Nice along the coastline.

Typical journeys between Menton and Nice-Ville take around 35–45 minutes, depending on the service and stops.

But a journey time isn't a timetable.

During February, both festivals attract large crowds and can affect access around railway stations, streets and event enclosures.

### Travelling to Nice

For most visitors without a car, rail is the first option to investigate.

Check the actual train to Nice-Ville or another suitable station.

Allow extra walking time to reach the Carnival security perimeter around Place Masséna and the surrounding streets.

### Returning after an evening parade

This is the most important part of the journey.

Check the actual departure available after the performance ends.

Don't assume that frequent daytime trains imply equally frequent late-night services.

SNCF currently publishes a Nice–Ventimiglia closure in both directions from 22:00 to 06:00 on Sunday–Thursday nights through 10 December 2027. This affects the normal rail return after the Tuesday 23 February 20:30–22:00 corso. Do not plan to catch a train after it: arrange a verified non-rail return or stay overnight in Nice. The Saturday 20 example is outside that recurring works pattern, but its actual services and any exceptions still need checking. Replacement buses are not an assumed direct Nice–Menton shuttle.

For late events, identify a suitable backup such as a prearranged transfer or overnight stay in Nice.

### Travelling by car

A car usually doesn't simplify the busiest festival days.

Both cities can have road closures, restricted access and heavy traffic.

If you're staying in central Menton and travelling only to Nice for Carnival, public transport is often more convenient, provided the return service is confirmed.

## 10. How much time should you allow for each event?

Festival days involve more than the official performance duration.

There may be queues, bag checks, barriers and slow movement through crowded areas.

| Experience | Reasonable planning allowance |
| --- | --- |
| Menton citrus exhibition | 1–2 hours |
| Menton daytime parade | Half-day including arrival and departure |
| Menton night parade | Full evening |
| Nice Flower Parade | Half-day including transport and access |
| Nice illuminated Carnival | Full evening, with return plan |
| Rest day in Menton | Entirely flexible |

These are itinerary allowances, not guaranteed admission or performance durations.

An organised crowd event may also require arriving earlier than you would for an ordinary museum visit.

For the Menton Sunday parades, official guidance advises visitors travelling into town to arrive before 11 a.m.

For the Thursday night parades, it recommends arriving before 6 p.m.

## 11. What if the weather changes?

February weather on the Riviera can be pleasantly sunny, but it isn't always warm or dry.

Rain, wind and unexpected weather alerts can affect outdoor performances.

The sensible solution is not to plan every day to the minute.

### Build flexibility into your accommodation dates

A five- or six-night stay gives you more opportunity to adjust plans than a two-night weekend.

However, don't assume that event tickets can automatically be transferred to another date.

Each organiser has its own rules for cancellations, refunds and rescheduling.

### Keep one indoor alternative

In Menton, consider a museum, café, cinema or an afternoon at the apartment.

In Nice, museums and indoor cultural attractions may offer an alternative to a disrupted outdoor event.

Check actual opening schedules before travelling.

### Don't replace a cancelled major event with another exhausting excursion

If a parade is cancelled, you may prefer a quiet afternoon rather than immediately boarding another train.

The point of a longer trip is having enough flexibility to enjoy the holiday even when the programme changes.

## 12. What about restaurants and groceries?

Festival days can make ordinary arrangements more complicated.

Restaurants near parade routes may be busy, and movement through central streets can take longer than usual.

### In Menton

If you're staying in an apartment, buy breakfast ingredients and simple meals before the busiest parade days.

The covered market, bakeries and supermarkets can provide convenient supplies, subject to their actual schedules.

Consider an early lunch before the Sunday Corso.

On night-parade Thursdays, plan dinner well ahead of the evening crowds.

### In Nice

If you have tickets for an afternoon event, decide where you'll eat beforehand.

Don't assume you can arrive at a popular restaurant immediately after the parade without waiting.

For a long Carnival day, a reservation can be useful.

### The value of an apartment

A kitchen doesn't mean you have to cook every day.

It means you can eat breakfast without rushing, make an early dinner before an event or enjoy a relaxed meal after a crowded afternoon.

That flexibility is particularly valuable with children.

## 13. A realistic budget for a 4–6 night festival trip

Festival travel costs more than a normal quiet winter stay if you attend several ticketed performances.

Accommodation rates can also increase during major event dates.

Rather than publishing a single unverified package price, separate your main expenses.

| Expense | How to budget |
| --- | --- |
| Accommodation | Request the total for exact festival dates |
| Menton tickets | Select individual confirmed activities |
| Nice Carnival tickets | Check event and seating category |
| Trains | Price the actual journeys and return times |
| Restaurants | Allow more for busy event evenings |
| Groceries | Budget for apartment breakfasts and simple meals |
| Transfers | Keep a reserve for a late return or disruption |

For a couple, two major ticketed performances, one additional exhibition and a few return train journeys can produce a meaningful extra cost beyond normal everyday spending.

For families, ticket prices and age-based eligibility should be calculated separately for every child.

Add a contingency for changes to transport or dining arrangements.

Don't spend the entire budget on non-refundable events without leaving room for an alternative plan.

## 14. What should you pack?

February on the Riviera calls for layers.

A sunny afternoon can be comfortable, while sitting outside during an evening parade may feel cold.

Bring a warm jacket, comfortable shoes, rain protection and something suitable for standing or sitting outdoors for an extended period.

For families, pack additional warm layers for children.

Check the event rules for bags, umbrellas, folding chairs and other items before arrival.

A small power bank can be useful for digital tickets, photographs and train information.

## 15. A final checklist before booking

Before committing to your February trip, confirm the following:

- Your dates overlap both festivals.

- You have selected a confirmed Menton event.

- Your chosen Nice Carnival event has an official date and time.

- You know which tickets require reserved seating.

- Your accommodation is convenient for your most important late event.

- You've checked transport back to Menton after any Nice evening performance.

- You have at least one quieter day in the itinerary.

- Your children can comfortably manage the chosen event times.

- You understand the ticket cancellation conditions.

- You have a weather and transport backup.

If one of the main event dates isn't confirmed yet, keep that part of the plan provisional.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can you visit the Menton Lemon Festival and Nice Carnival in one trip?

Yes. Their dates overlap in February 2027, making it possible to attend both during a four- to six-night holiday.

### When is the best time to visit both festivals in 2027?

The period from 18 to 23 February is a strong option because it includes confirmed Menton events on 18 and 21 February. Choose your Nice event after verifying its exact programme.

### Is four nights enough?

Yes, if you focus on one major event in each city and leave some time for rest. Five nights provides more flexibility.

### Should you stay in Menton or Nice?

Menton is better suited to travellers who prioritise the Lemon Festival and prefer quieter evenings. Nice is more convenient if you intend to attend several late-night Carnival events.

### Can you travel between Nice and Menton without a car?

Yes. Regional trains connect the cities. Check actual departures, especially when returning after an evening parade.

### Is this trip suitable for children?

Yes, with an age-appropriate itinerary. Daytime events, rest periods and manageable walking distances are particularly important for younger children. Our family example is 18–23 February, with the Nice flower parade on 20 February and an optional Menton parade on 21 February, subject to suitable tickets.

### Do you need separate tickets for both festivals?

Yes. The Lemon Festival and Nice Carnival have different organisers and ticketing systems.

### Should you book accommodation before tickets?

Ideally, coordinate both bookings once your desired event dates are confirmed. Avoid non-refundable arrangements based on an unconfirmed Carnival session.

### What happens if a parade is cancelled?

Check the organiser's official cancellation and refund policy. Don't assume that your ticket can automatically be used on another date.

### Can you visit both festivals in a single day?

Geographically, it may be possible for certain combinations of events. But it's usually less enjoyable than spreading the experiences across several days, especially with children.

## Final thoughts: two festivals, one Riviera holiday

The Menton Lemon Festival and Nice Carnival are among the French Riviera's most distinctive winter events.

One celebrates a small coastal town's remarkable citrus heritage through enormous sculptures and colourful parades.

The other transforms a major Mediterranean city into a theatre of costumes, music, satire and spectacle.

They make an extraordinary combination.

But the best way to enjoy them isn't to race between cities and attend every available event.

Choose a few memorable performances.

Give yourself time to explore Menton and Nice outside the festival enclosures.

Take a proper day off between crowded experiences.

And choose accommodation that makes the return journey easy.

With four to six nights, you can experience two great Riviera celebrations while still enjoying the Mediterranean atmosphere that makes February here so special.
