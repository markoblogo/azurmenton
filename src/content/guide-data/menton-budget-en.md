# How Much Does a Holiday in Menton Cost? 3, 7 and 14-Day Budgets







Menton has a rather pleasant financial advantage over some of its more glamorous neighbours on the French Riviera: you can spend surprisingly little here, or considerably more, and have a wonderful holiday either way.

A morning croissant, a swim at Les Sablettes and a picnic overlooking the Mediterranean might cost less than €20. On another day, you could enjoy a long seafood lunch, rent a sunbed, order cocktails and finish with dinner overlooking the sea. That could easily become a €150 day.

Both are perfectly normal ways to experience Menton.

So how much spending money should you actually bring for three days, a week or a fortnight?

We've put together three realistic budgets, covering groceries, restaurants, local transport, excursions and entertainment. Accommodation is excluded, making the figures useful whether you're renting an apartment, staying in a hotel or visiting friends.

## Menton holiday budget at a glance

Budget (€40/day): Supermarket breakfasts, simple local lunches, occasional restaurant meals, public beaches and mostly free activities.

Comfortable (€85/day): A mixture of groceries and restaurants, proper dinners, café stops, occasional drinks, day trips and paid attractions.

Premium experiences (€155/day): Better restaurants, cocktails, private beach days, cultural experiences, more flexible transport and room for a few memorable splurges.

These are planning allowances, not promises that every day will cost the same. Some days might cost €25, while others reach €200.

And you don't have to choose one category for your entire holiday. Many visitors spend modestly most days, then reserve extra money for something special.

## 1. Where does the money actually go?

Before choosing a budget, it helps to understand what you're paying for.

| Expense | Budget/day | Comfortable/day | Premium/day |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Groceries, breakfast and snacks | €13 | €15 | €18 |
| Restaurants, cafés and drinks | €18 | €45 | €90 |
| Local transport and day trips | €3 | €8 | €12 |
| Attractions, beaches and activities | €6 | €17 | €35 |
| Total per day | €40 | €85 | €155 |

The biggest difference between these travel styles isn't transport or sightseeing. It's how often you eat out, where you choose to eat and how much you spend on optional experiences.

Menton itself is wonderfully walkable. The sea, the old town, the market and many cafés are close together. You can have an enjoyable day without buying an attraction ticket or using public transport.

Food and lifestyle choices are what really change the final bill.

## 2. Food prices in Menton: from supermarket picnics to long dinners

The easiest way to control holiday spending in Menton is through food. But there's no reason to survive on supermarket sandwiches for a week unless that's genuinely how you like to eat.

Here are practical price ranges to use when planning.

### Groceries, bakeries and quick meals

| Item | Typical planning allowance |
| --- | --- |

| Espresso | €2–3 |

| Groceries for a simple apartment dinner | €7–14 per person |

These are approximate shopping and menu allowances rather than verified prices at every business.

For guests with a kitchen, Menton's markets and supermarkets make a real difference.

Fresh tomatoes, mozzarella, bread, olives, fruit and a few local specialties can become an excellent Mediterranean lunch without a restaurant bill.

The covered market, Halles du Marché, is especially enjoyable in the morning. You can shop for picnic ingredients, try regional products and get a little more out of the experience than a routine grocery run.

For a proper stock-up, larger supermarkets are generally more practical than small convenience shops in the tourist centre.

### Restaurants: what should you realistically expect?

Menton has everything from takeaway pizza to serious fine dining. A lunch special is useful, but it shouldn't define your entire holiday budget.

| Restaurant experience | Approximate cost per person |
| --- | --- |
| Pizza, pasta or simple casual meal | €15–25 |
| Casual lunch with a drink | €20–35 |
| Full lunch at a mid-range restaurant | €30–50 |
| Dinner with a main, dessert and drink | €35–60 |
| Better seafood or Mediterranean dinner | €50–85 |
| More elaborate dinner with wine | €80–120+ |
| Fine-dining tasting experience | €150–300+ |

A quick restaurant lunch and a three-course dinner are completely different expenses.

Consider a typical comfortable holiday day:

- Breakfast in the apartment: €5

- Coffee and pastry later: €6

- Restaurant lunch: €23

- Afternoon ice cream: €5

- Dinner with a glass of wine: €40

That's €79 before transport or entertainment.

You wouldn't necessarily eat this way every day. But it's an entirely believable holiday day, and it explains why a comfortable Riviera trip costs more than the cheapest lunch prices might suggest.

For a week-long stay, mixing restaurant days with market meals is often the most enjoyable approach.

## 3. Transport: do you actually need a car?

For most visitors staying near central Menton, the answer is no.

You can walk from the seafront to the old town, cafés, beaches and railway station. Regional trains make Monaco, Nice and Ventimiglia accessible without driving or looking for parking.

### Useful 2026 transport prices

| Transport | Price |
| --- | --- |
| Zest local bus single ticket | €2 |
| Menton local shuttle | €1 |
| Zest one-day ticket | €5 |
| Zest seven-day ticket | €13 |
| Pass Sud Azur tourist, 3 days | €35 |
| Pass Sud Azur tourist, 7 days | €50 |
| Pass Sud Azur tourist, 14 days | €80 |

These fares were checked against Zest and TER Sud official pages on 4 November 2026. The Zest single ticket is valid for one hour with connections but no return journey; specified exceptions apply. La Navette is a separate €1 product.

The Pass Sud Azur tourist product covers participating bus, tram and train services across the relevant Alpes-Maritimes and Monaco networks. Check the exact zone and operator validity before purchase. Do not assume it covers travel into Italy: the listed coastal train scope ends at Menton Garavan. Keep a separate allowance for any journey beyond the pass's validity.

A pass isn't automatically cheaper than individual tickets.

For example, if you're spending most of your week walking around Menton, a seven-day regional pass may be unnecessary. But if you're repeatedly visiting Nice, Monaco and other Riviera destinations, it may become worthwhile.

For occasional rail trips, compare individual TER tickets through SNCF Connect. Fares depend on the route and applicable conditions, so we haven't presented a single unverified train price as universal.

Our advice: Don't rent a car for an entire week just because you're visiting the French Riviera. Consider one only for particular trips where public transport would be inconvenient.

## 4. Beaches, museums and entertainment

Some of Menton's best experiences are free.

Walking through the pastel-coloured old town, watching the sea from the promenade, swimming at a public beach and exploring the coastal scenery require little more than comfortable shoes and a little curiosity.

But additional experiences deserve a place in a realistic holiday budget.

Private-beach and other activity amounts are planning allowances, not verified current venue quotations; seasonal opening, reservation, front-row charges and inclusions need checking.

### Is a private beach worth the money?

Sometimes, absolutely.

If you're visiting Menton in summer, a reserved sunbed and parasol can make a hot day significantly more comfortable.

But you don't need to pay €40 every time you want to swim.

Try a free public beach on most days and perhaps one full-service beach day during a longer holiday.

This is a good example of how a comfortable trip can include premium experiences without requiring a premium budget every day.

## 5. Three days in Menton: how much spending money?

Three days is enough to discover the old town, enjoy the beaches and get a taste of the local food.

Because the stay is short, you may spend more per day than on a longer holiday, especially if you want to fit several paid experiences into a weekend.

Budget: €120 per person

Enjoy bakery breakfasts, one simple restaurant meal most days, supermarket picnics and free attractions. Spend time at Les Sablettes, visit the old town and walk along the promenade.

Comfortable: €255 per person

Have coffee at a terrace, enjoy several proper restaurant meals, visit a garden or museum and take a short excursion. You have room for cocktails, desserts and little unplanned pleasures.

Premium: €465 per person

Combine memorable restaurant meals with a private beach visit, more drinks and paid activities. You can eat very well without having to calculate every euro.

Suggested three-day rhythm: Old town and seaside dining on day one, beach and gardens on day two, then a relaxed excursion or market morning on day three.

## 6. Seven days in Menton: the ideal balance

A week gives you time to explore without feeling that every day needs to be packed with activities.

And it allows you to alternate inexpensive beach days with outings and better restaurant experiences.

Budget: €280 per person

Shop for breakfasts and some dinners, eat casually, explore on foot and choose one or two inexpensive attractions or day trips.

Comfortable: €595 per person

Enjoy restaurant lunches or dinners regularly, explore Menton's gardens, take trips to nearby destinations and include several cultural activities.

Premium: €1,085 per person

Add multiple restaurant experiences, a beach club visit or two, quality seafood dinners, drinks and a selection of excursions and special activities.

### An example of a comfortable week

You might spend:

| Category | Weekly allowance |
| --- | --- |
| Groceries, breakfasts and snacks | €105 |
| Restaurants, cafés and drinks | €315 |
| Local transport and excursions | €56 |
| Activities and entertainment | €119 |

This doesn't mean spending €45 in restaurants every single day.

You could have a €12 picnic lunch one day, a €70 dinner another evening and a few meals in between.

The weekly total is what matters.

With seven days, consider mixing Menton itself with one or two trips to Monaco, Ventimiglia, Roquebrune-Cap-Martin or Nice.

You can also leave an entire day unplanned. Sometimes that's the best day of a Riviera holiday.

## 7. Fourteen days: how to avoid doubling every expense

Two weeks in Menton shouldn't necessarily feel like two expensive one-week holidays.

Longer stays make it easier to establish a routine. You discover your preferred bakery, find a supermarket, cook a little more often and stop treating every lunch as an occasion.

Our baseline figures are:

| Style | 14-day allowance |
| --- | --- |
| Budget | €560 |
| Comfortable | €1,190 |
| Premium experiences | €2,170 |

These are deliberately calculated from the same daily spending assumptions, so the comparisons remain consistent.

In reality, there may be opportunities to spend less.

You might buy a larger grocery supply, use a transport pass more efficiently or spend more days enjoying free activities.

On the other hand, a fortnight offers more opportunities for boat trips, expensive restaurants, live events and excursions.

### What we'd recommend for two weeks

Spend the first several days exploring Menton itself. Discover the market, local restaurants, old-town streets and beaches.

Use the middle of the holiday for excursions. Monaco, Nice, Ventimiglia and other nearby destinations provide plenty of variety.

Save the final few days for your favourites.

Return to that restaurant you loved. Spend another afternoon by the sea. Have a long lunch without watching the clock.

The advantage of a longer holiday is having enough time not to rush.

## 8. How we calculated these budgets

It's important to explain what these numbers represent.

Model reference date: 27 October 2026; official transport/garden tariffs reviewed 4 November 2026.

Currency: Euros, per adult.

Accommodation: Not included.

Calculation method: We divided typical holiday spending into four categories: groceries, restaurants and drinks, local transport, and paid activities.

We then assigned a daily allowance to each category for the three travel styles.

The formula is:

Holiday budget = number of days × (daily groceries + restaurants and drinks + transport + activities).

For example, the comfortable seven-day budget is:

7 × (€15 + €45 + €8 + €17) = €595.

These allowances combine published prices for specific services with estimated spending patterns. They are not an average derived from a representative survey of Menton visitors.

Official transport fares and the garden's published tariffs were rechecked with the operators. Private beach, restaurant and shopping amounts remain editorial planning ranges, not verified quotes. The garden's published seasonal closure takes precedence over its generic daily-hours panel.

Restaurant and supermarket figures are indicative ranges. Menus, seasonal offers, wine choices, location and shopping habits can change the actual bill substantially.

### What's included?

- Everyday groceries, breakfasts and snacks

- Restaurant meals, cafés and drinks

- Local public transport and an allowance for nearby excursions

- Paid attractions, museums, gardens and beach facilities

- Everyday entertainment spending

### What's excluded?

- Accommodation

- Flights and long-distance travel to the Riviera

- Airport transfers

- Car rental, fuel, tolls and parking

- Shopping, clothing and substantial souvenirs

- Travel insurance

- Major festival packages and expensive special events

- Exceptional fine-dining splurges beyond the stated daily allowance

If you plan several Michelin-starred meals, private boat charters or frequent taxis, calculate those separately.

For a safer budget, consider adding 10–15% contingency on top of the totals, particularly during the busy summer season.

## 9. Is Menton cheaper than Monaco or Nice?

This is not a statistically verified city-price comparison. Menton can suit a lower-spend itinerary when your day centres on markets, casual meals, walks and public beaches; a similar basket and travel dates would be needed to compare it fairly with Monaco.

Compared with Nice, the answer is less straightforward.

Both cities offer supermarkets, bakeries, affordable meals and higher-priced restaurants. Nice has a broader range of entertainment and dining, while Menton's compact centre can reduce your need for paid transport.

The important distinction isn't that everything in Menton is cheap.

It's that you can enjoy so much of what makes the town attractive without buying many tickets or travelling far.

The most beautiful view in Menton might cost nothing.

The dinner you order while admiring it is another matter.

## 10. How to get better value without compromising your holiday

The most effective savings often come from a few small decisions rather than aggressive cost-cutting.

Have breakfast at home sometimes. A coffee and pastry on a café terrace is lovely, but doing it every morning adds up, particularly for families.

Shop at the market and supermarket. Fresh local food isn't a consolation prize for not eating out. It can be one of the best parts of staying in Menton.

Spend your restaurant budget where it matters. Choose a restaurant because you genuinely want its food, atmosphere or view, not simply because it's nearby.

Walk whenever practical. Menton rewards slow exploration and has relatively short distances between many central attractions.

Use regional trains thoughtfully. Monaco, Nice and Ventimiglia are attractive excursions, but you don't need to leave Menton every day.

Choose paid experiences selectively. One memorable beach club afternoon may be more satisfying than renting a sunbed every day.

Mix your travel styles. A budget lunch and a wonderful dinner can belong to the same holiday.

For a family staying in an apartment, preparing breakfast and some evening meals can make a significant difference over a week or two.

## 12. So, how much should you budget for Menton?

For a first visit, we'd generally suggest around €85 per adult per day, excluding accommodation, as a comfortable starting point.

That's enough to enjoy restaurants, cafés, local food, some attractions and the occasional Riviera excursion without approaching every purchase as an exercise in cost control.

If you enjoy simple meals, public beaches and walking, €40 a day is a workable budget target, although you'll need to make deliberate choices.

And if your perfect Riviera holiday involves long restaurant lunches, beach clubs, cocktails and memorable experiences, €155 a day is a more realistic starting point. It isn't a ceiling.

The best thing about Menton is that you don't have to spend extravagantly to enjoy it.

But you also don't have to pretend that a holiday is just a series of cheap lunches.

Choose the experiences that matter to you, leave some room for spontaneity and enjoy the Mediterranean at your own pace.
