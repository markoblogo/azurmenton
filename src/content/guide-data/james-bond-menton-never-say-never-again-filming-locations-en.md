# James Bond in Menton: The Never Say Never Again Location Walk

James Bond has travelled to some extraordinary places. He has driven along mountain roads, escaped from elaborate traps, visited spectacular casinos and pursued dangerous enemies through cities that rarely seem designed for ordinary traffic. But one of his more unusual adventures takes place in the narrow, stepped streets of Menton.

In Never Say Never Again, the 1983 James Bond film starring Sean Connery, Bond finds himself pursued by Fatima Blush, played by Barbara Carrera. The chase takes him through the eastern French Riviera on a specially equipped motorcycle. And for part of that sequence, the production chose Menton.

The motorcycle passes through narrow streets and down the remarkable stone steps of the old town, before the action continues towards a road tunnel. On screen, everything happens at breathtaking speed. In reality, these are streets, historic buildings and public spaces that deserve a much slower visit.

This guide explores the places behind the chase, explains how the filmmakers connected different towns into one continuous action sequence and offers a safe walking route through the publicly accessible parts of Menton. You won't need a motorcycle. In fact, we strongly recommend leaving it behind.

## 1. The film: Never Say Never Again

Title: Never Say Never Again Released: 1983 Director: Irvin Kershner James Bond: Sean Connery Fatima Blush: Barbara Carrera Domino Petachi: Kim Basinger Maximilian Largo: Klaus Maria Brandauer Approximate running time: 134 minutes The film marked Sean Connery's return to the role of James Bond after his earlier appearances in the series.

It is a new adaptation of material previously used for Thunderball (1965), but was produced outside the regular Eon Productions series. That makes it something of an unusual entry in Bond history. The familiar elements are present: international intrigue, a criminal organisation, a formidable villain, spectacular locations and a succession of elaborate action scenes.

The French Riviera provides some of the film's most recognisable European settings. Monte Carlo, Menton and Villefranche-sur-Mer appear in the production, with additional locations elsewhere in France and beyond. For visitors staying in Menton, the motorcycle chase is the most interesting part to investigate.

## 3. Stop One: above Menton, where the old town becomes a film set

Area: Cimetière du Vieux Château and the upper historic centre. Begin above Menton's old town. From this part of the hill, the city's rooftops descend towards the Mediterranean, with the coastline stretching towards Italy and Monaco. Today, it's one of Menton's most atmospheric viewpoints.

The Vieux Château cemetery occupies the site of the town's former castle. Its historic tombs tell the story of the international community that came to Menton during the nineteenth and early twentieth centuries. The surrounding streets also provide a useful introduction to the geography of the Bond chase.

### Why does this setting work on screen?

Menton's upper town is built on a steep hillside. Its narrow streets, stone walls and steps create dramatic changes in elevation. For filmmakers, these features provide natural visual excitement. A camera can look downhill along a stairway, revealing buildings and the sea beyond. A motorcycle entering that space immediately appears to be doing something unexpected. The place itself provides much of the spectacle.

### Visiting responsibly

The cemetery is an active place of remembrance. Follow its published opening arrangements, remain on public paths and respect graves and other visitors. There is no need to climb onto walls or reconstruct the film's camera positions in restricted areas. Film connection: Upper old-town environment associated with the Menton chase, rather than a separately verified cemetery-interior shot.

## 4. Stop Two: Rampes Saint-Michel, the most recognisable Bond location

Location: Historic steps near Basilique Saint-Michel-Archange. This is the central stop of the guide. In the film, Bond rides his motorcycle down a steep flight of steps beside Menton's historic architecture. The sequence is a wonderful example of how an ordinary pedestrian space can become extraordinary in an action film.

The stairs offer a dramatic perspective, with buildings rising on either side and the Mediterranean town appearing beyond.

### What to compare with the film

Specialist location research identifies the motorcycle descent beside Saint-Michel at approximately 1:23:28. Look for the relationship between the stairway, adjacent walls, buildings and visible architectural details. Remember that the precise camera frame may exclude much of the surrounding space.

And while the historic setting remains recognisable, individual façades, surfaces and street furnishings may have changed since filming.

### The real architecture

Saint-Michel is one of Menton's most significant historic landmarks. The basilica's baroque architecture, bell tower and elevated square dominate the old-town skyline. Its appearance gives the screen sequence an unmistakably Mediterranean character. The production did not need to construct a fictional old town. Menton already provided one.

### Visiting information

The public stairs can be explored on foot. They are steep, and their stone surfaces may be slippery after rain. Wear appropriate shoes. Visitors with limited mobility should not treat this as an easy walking route. Important: The motorcycle descent is a professional stunt. The steps are not an appropriate place to ride a motorcycle, bicycle or scooter.

## 5. Stop Three: Saint-Michel Square, the architecture behind the action

Location: Parvis Saint-Michel. After exploring the steps, take time to appreciate the basilica square itself. The architecture is worth visiting independently of the Bond connection. The historic paving, church façades and views over the rooftops create one of the most remarkable urban spaces in Menton.

### Why stop here?

The film turns the area into a place of speed and danger. As a visitor, you can experience the opposite. Stand in the square and look back towards the steps. Notice how the buildings frame the route. Think about where a camera might be positioned to make the descent feel especially dramatic.

### A simple frame-matching exercise

When comparing a still from the film with the present-day square, look for stable architectural elements:

- The alignment of the steps.

- The corners of adjacent buildings.

- Door and window openings.

- The relationship between the wall and stairway.

- Background rooflines.

Don't rely entirely on paint colours, shop signs or surface details that may have changed. This is how filming-location identification becomes more reliable than simply recognising a picturesque street.

## 6. Stop Four: Rue Longue, a street built for close-up cinema

Location: Rue Longue, Menton's old town. From Saint-Michel, continue through publicly accessible historic lanes towards Rue Longue. This narrow street preserves much of the atmosphere that makes Menton an attractive filming location. The façades sit close together. Small passages and stairways connect different levels of the town. Sudden changes in light create strong contrasts between bright walls and shaded corners.

### The filming connection

AzurMenton's existing filming guide includes Rue Longue within the Menton motorcycle-chase sequence. However, a precise shot-by-shot match for this particular street has not yet been independently completed. That distinction matters. It's reasonable to include the street within the existing editorial location walk.

[Riviera on Film's comparison catalogue](https://rivieraonfilm.wixsite.com/riviera-on-film/never-say-never-again-1983) also identifies Rue Longue near Hôtel Pretti. That provides an external location lead, not a claim that AzurMenton has independently matched every frame.

It's less reasonable to claim that a particular doorway or exact corner appears at a specific second without visual confirmation.

### What makes the street interesting anyway?

Rue Longue is a valuable part of Menton's older urban landscape. Its close architectural perspectives, stone surfaces and warm-coloured buildings give visitors a sense of the compact historic centre that existed long before modern seafront development. Even without its cinema connection, it's worth exploring. Visiting advice: Remain on public paths and avoid blocking entrances or photographing residents without permission.

## 7. Stop Five: Place du Cap, where the old town meets the waterfront

Location: Place du Cap. As you descend through the old town, the urban environment begins to open. The closely packed historic lanes give way to lower streets and the waterfront. Place du Cap is a useful transition point. It allows you to appreciate just how compressed Menton's historic centre is. Within a short distance, the route moves from high stone stairways to the harbour district.

### Why does this matter in the film?

Action scenes are often constructed through editing. The audience sees a turn, a descent, a vehicle passing through a street and a cut to another angle. The sequence feels continuous because the movement and visual rhythm fit together. But the exact geography on screen doesn't necessarily match the most practical route through the real town.

Place du Cap helps visitors understand how these different spaces relate to one another.

### The location evidence

The existing AzurMenton guide places Place du Cap within its Bond location sequence. For a definitive frame-by-frame article, the next step would be to identify the exact shot showing the square and compare it with current photographs. Until then, it is best described as a documented itinerary stop rather than a confirmed camera position.

Riviera on Film labels a comparison at the Rue des Logettes / Place du Cap / Quai Bonaparte junction. Treat that as an externally documented location, while keeping the precise modern camera position unverified here.

## 8. Stop Six: Quai Bonaparte, the Riviera opens towards the sea

Location: Quai Bonaparte, near the old port. The old-town walk can finish along the waterfront. Here, Menton feels very different from its steep upper streets. The sea opens ahead, the harbour becomes visible and the old buildings rise behind the promenade.

### What to see

Walk along the public pavement. Look towards the Bastion, the old port and the colourful historic façades. The setting combines several qualities that filmmakers associate with the Riviera: water, historic architecture, sunlight and compact urban streets.

### Film connection

AzurMenton's location guide includes Quai Bonaparte as part of the lower transition within the Menton Bond sequence. Exact camera positions remain to be matched independently.

### A good place to finish

This is an excellent stopping point for the visitor route. You can enjoy the harbour, have a drink or continue towards Esplanade des Sablettes. The walk is rewarding even if you're not trying to reproduce every movement seen on screen.

## 9. The tunnel: an important filming location, not a walking stop

Location: Square Victoria and the Pascal-Molinari tunnel area. One of the most memorable parts of the chase involves Bond entering a tunnel. Independent filming research identifies the location at Square Victoria in Menton, at approximately 1:23:46 in its referenced film version. This is a genuine part of the filming-location story.

But it should not be included as a pedestrian destination inside the tunnel or along its traffic approaches.

### Why isn't it part of the walking route?

The Pascal-Molinari tunnel is a road facility. It carries vehicle traffic and is associated with a complex road junction. The surrounding road layout has also changed since the film was made. Municipal reports describe a major reconfiguration of the Square Victoria junction and tunnel exit during works beginning in 2023.

For a visitor, trying to recreate the filming position by walking towards the tunnel entrance would be inappropriate and potentially dangerous.

### What can you do instead?

Study the film stills and compare them with historical photographs or maps. If you're interested in road and urban history, the City of Menton's published documentation explains how the tunnel junction has changed. Don't enter the tunnel on foot, stop on the carriageway or use traffic lanes to recreate the scene. Filming landmark only. Not part of the recommended walk.

## 10. Beyond Menton: the chase continues in Villefranche-sur-Mer

The most fascinating geographical twist comes after Menton. The production used Villefranche-sur-Mer for the continuation and culmination of the motorcycle pursuit. On screen, the action feels like one fast-moving sequence. In reality, Menton and Villefranche-sur-Mer are separate towns along the coast, with Monaco and other communities between them.

### Port de la Darse and the old harbour

The specialist filming site Riviera on Film identifies a warehouse at the Darse harbour as the location associated with the end of the motorcycle chase involving Bond and Fatima Blush. Independent scene research places the warehouse sequence around 1:25:37, at Quai de la Corderie. The harbour's old maritime buildings, stonework and industrial structures provide a very different atmosphere from the colourful Menton old town.

### Why this location works

The Menton sequence relies on steep urban geography. The Villefranche sequence uses the working harbour environment. The combination creates variety and a sense of escalating action. Editing connects them into what feels like a continuous pursuit. But a viewer shouldn't use the screen sequence to infer the real distance between locations.

### A separate half-day excursion

Take a regional train from Menton towards Villefranche-sur-Mer. Check your departure and return services for the actual date. From Villefranche-sur-Mer station, explore the waterfront and historic harbour surroundings using public pedestrian routes. The Darse harbour is a working maritime area, so some facilities and sections may be restricted. Remain on publicly accessible paths.

Don't enter warehouses, workshops or operational port areas without authorisation.

### Another Villefranche connection

The regional tourist office also identifies Quai de l'Amiral Courbet as a location where scenes involving Largo's yacht were filmed. This is a separate part of the film's Riviera geography. It's a useful addition to a general Bond-themed visit, but should not be confused with the warehouse at Quai de la Corderie.

## 11. Which locations were Menton, Monaco and Villefranche?

The French Riviera appears relatively compact on screen. In reality, the film used several different places.

| Real location | Film connection | How to treat it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Menton | Old-town motorcycle chase and tunnel sequence | Main walking guide and separately documented road landmark |
| Villefranche-sur-Mer | Harbour, warehouse, yacht and coastal scenes | Separate half-day visit |
| Monte Carlo, Monaco | Casino scenes and glamorous Riviera setting | Separate city and separate filming context |
| Other locations | Studio work, action sequences and international settings | Not part of this Menton walk |

This distinction prevents a common mistake. A sequence presented as occurring around Monaco or along the Riviera may have been assembled from footage shot in different municipalities. The geographical continuity belongs to the editing. The real filming locations remain separate.

## 12. Then and now: how to compare the chase with today's Menton

For serious filming-location enthusiasts, the most useful approach is to compare the original scene with the same public space today. Here are three practical reference points.

| Scene | Published approximate timestamp | What to compare |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Motorcycle down Saint-Michel steps | 1:23:28 | Stair geometry, walls, façades |
| Entrance towards the tunnel | 1:23:46 | Arch, road alignment and historical surroundings; desk research only |
| Warehouse chase in Villefranche | 1:25:37 | Historic harbour buildings and waterfront layout |

Timestamps are taken from an independent location catalogue and may vary between editions, streaming versions or playback formats. They have not yet been verified independently against a licensed copy of the film.

### How to make a good comparison photograph

Find a safe, public pedestrian position. Use the main architectural features to establish the perspective. Try to match the approximate viewing direction and framing. A phone camera is usually sufficient. If the old scene used an elevated position, do not climb walls or enter restricted property to reproduce it.

Some historical comparisons are better made using publicly available photographs rather than attempting the original camera position.

### What may have changed since 1983?

Shopfronts. Road signs. Street furniture. Paving and pedestrian arrangements. Traffic systems. And, around Square Victoria, substantial road infrastructure. A location doesn't need to look identical to remain historically identifiable.

## 13. The safe Bond walking itinerary

For a visitor staying in Menton, this is the route we recommend. Start: Upper old town, near Saint-Michel. Finish: Quai Bonaparte and the old port. Allow: Approximately 60–90 minutes, with extra time if you visit the basilica or cemetery.

| Stop | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| 1. Upper old town | Enjoy the historic setting and views |
| 2. Rampes Saint-Michel | Compare the famous motorcycle-stairway scene |
| 3. Saint-Michel square | Examine the architecture and filming perspective |
| 4. Rue Longue | Explore the narrow historic streets |
| 5. Place du Cap | Notice the transition from the old town towards the port |
| 6. Quai Bonaparte | Finish with the waterfront and old harbour |

The walk is intentionally arranged for pedestrians. It does not attempt to reproduce the speed, road movements or exact order of the motorcycle chase.

### Accessibility

The full route includes substantial steps and steep historic streets. It is unsuitable as a step-free itinerary. Visitors who prefer a gentler outing can instead enjoy the lower old-town and waterfront areas, without climbing to Saint-Michel.

### With children

Older children and teenagers interested in Bond films may particularly enjoy comparing the locations with on-screen stills. However, the stairway section requires appropriate supervision. The visit should be a normal historical walking activity, not an attempt to imitate stunts.

## Frequently asked questions

### Was Never Say Never Again filmed in Menton?

Yes. Official regional tourism information identifies Menton as a filming location, while independent databases associate it with the motorcycle chase.

### Where are the famous James Bond stairs in Menton?

The motorcycle descent is associated with the steps beside Basilique Saint-Michel-Archange in the historic old town.

### Can you walk down the same steps?

You can use the public pedestrian steps under ordinary access conditions, but they are steep. The film's stunt should not be recreated.

### Where was the tunnel scene filmed?

Specialist filming research identifies the Square Victoria and Pascal-Molinari tunnel area in Menton. It is a road landmark, not a pedestrian visitor route.

### Was the chase filmed entirely in Menton?

No. The sequence also uses Villefranche-sur-Mer, including a warehouse in the Darse harbour area.

### Was the film shot in Monaco?

Yes, some casino scenes were filmed in Monte Carlo. But Menton and Villefranche-sur-Mer are distinct real filming locations.

### Is Never Say Never Again part of the official Eon Bond series?

No. It is an independently produced James Bond film featuring Sean Connery, outside the regular Eon Productions series.

### How long is the Menton Bond walk?

Allow approximately one to one and a half hours for the recommended old-town route, including stops for photographs.

### Is it suitable for wheelchair users?

The full Saint-Michel route is not step-free. Visitors with mobility limitations should choose the lower old-town and waterfront sections instead.

### Can I visit the Villefranche warehouse?

The harbour includes operational maritime facilities. Do not assume warehouse interiors are open to the public. Explore only publicly accessible areas.

### Can I match the exact film screenshots?

The Saint-Michel location and several wider locations have published scene references. A full verified then-and-now image comparison still requires frame-by-frame research.

## Final thoughts: the real Riviera behind the Bond chase

Watching the motorcycle chase in Never Say Never Again, it's easy to imagine one continuous pursuit through an elegant Mediterranean destination. But the real story of the filming is more interesting. The production used Menton's extraordinary old-town geography for the steep streets and steps. It used the road tunnel area for another moment of action.

Then it connected those images with harbour scenes filmed in Villefranche-sur-Mer. Monaco contributed another part of the film's glamorous Riviera setting. The result is a fictional geography assembled from real places. For travellers, the pleasure is discovering those places at a completely different speed. Walk through Menton's old town. Take time to admire Saint-Michel. Look out over the sea.

And remember that a staircase which now offers a beautiful view once carried one of cinema's most recognisable secret agents through an improbable motorcycle escape. The film lasts a little over two hours. The locations have stories that go back centuries.
