# Two to Four Weeks in Menton: How to Organise a Longer Stay

There's a point during a longer holiday in Menton when something rather pleasant happens.

You stop checking Google Maps every time you leave the apartment. You know which bakery makes the croissants you like. You've worked out where to buy tomatoes, which beach feels right in the afternoon and how long it takes to walk to the station.

You might even have a favourite bench.

At that point, you're no longer simply visiting Menton. You're beginning to live here, at least for a little while.

Two to four weeks is enough time to experience the French Riviera differently. You can explore Monaco, Nice and the Italian coast without sacrificing lazy mornings, ordinary routines or evenings spent cooking dinner in your apartment.

But a longer stay requires a little more organisation than a three-day holiday.

Here's how to make it work without turning your Mediterranean escape into a complicated logistical exercise.

## 1. Start with the apartment, not the itinerary

For a weekend, an attractive location and a comfortable bed might be sufficient.

For three or four weeks, the small practical details matter much more.

Before booking, think about how you'll actually spend an ordinary Tuesday.

Will you prepare breakfast? Work for several hours? Wash clothes? Eat lunch at home? Need somewhere to store groceries?

An apartment that looks wonderful in photographs isn't necessarily comfortable for these everyday activities.

### What matters during a longer stay?

| Feature | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| Functional kitchen | Makes regular meals and grocery shopping practical |
| Fridge with adequate space | Important for fresh produce and several days of supplies |
| Washing machine or laundry access | Reduces packing and weekly costs |
| Reliable Wi-Fi | Essential for remote work and everyday communication |
| Table and comfortable chair | Useful for working, eating and planning |
| Air conditioning or effective cooling | Particularly important in July and August |
| Heating | Worth confirming for winter stays |
| Walkable grocery shopping | Makes ordinary life significantly easier |
| Noise and light conditions | Important when working or sleeping regularly |
| Lift or manageable stairs | Particularly relevant with luggage, children or reduced mobility |
| Convenient rubbish disposal | A small but recurring part of apartment life |

Also confirm the actual kitchen equipment.

A kitchen may technically exist but contain little more than a microwave, two plates and a frying pan.

For a month-long stay, you'll probably want a saucepan, chopping board, sharp knife, enough utensils and somewhere sensible to dry dishes.

Our advice: Choose an apartment that works for everyday life, not just for the first twenty minutes after arrival.

## 2. Your first 48 hours: set up a comfortable routine

There's no need to plan a major excursion immediately after arriving.

Use the first two days to understand your neighbourhood and make the apartment feel like a temporary home.

### Arrival day

Unpack properly, rather than living out of a suitcase for three weeks.

Check the Wi-Fi, kitchen appliances, hot water, air conditioning or heating, and laundry facilities.

Find the nearest supermarket and buy enough for breakfast, drinks and one or two simple meals.

Keep your first dinner uncomplicated. You can eat out or make something easy in the apartment.

Also locate your nearest rubbish and recycling containers, and check the building's disposal instructions.

### Your first full day

Take a relaxed walk around the neighbourhood.

Find a bakery, pharmacy, supermarket, ATM if needed, and the most convenient route to the beach or station.

Make your first proper grocery purchase. Test the kitchen with a simple meal.

If you'll be working remotely, test the internet connection under normal working conditions, especially video calls if those are essential.

By the end of day two, you should have solved most of the practical problems that otherwise interrupt the rest of your holiday.

## 3. Grocery shopping: combine a market with supermarkets

One of the advantages of staying in Menton for several weeks is having time to shop like a resident.

You don't need to eat every meal in a restaurant, but you don't need to give up restaurants either.

A good routine combines a weekly supermarket visit, regular fresh-food shopping and the occasional bakery stop.

### Start with Menton's covered market

Halles Municipales, at Quai de Monléon, is an excellent place to buy fruit, vegetables, cheeses, bread, olives and regional ingredients.

The municipal market operates daily, generally in the morning, with the city publishing hours of 7:30 a.m. to 1 p.m. Individual stall availability varies, and Monday can be quieter.

It's particularly useful for buying ingredients for a lunch at home or a Mediterranean-style dinner.

For example:

- Fresh tomatoes and mozzarella

- Bread and local olive oil

- Seasonal fruit

- Olives, tapenade or other regional products

- Cheese and charcuterie

- Fresh ingredients for a simple pasta dish

You don't have to make the market your only source of food. Think of it as the enjoyable part of shopping.

### Use supermarkets for the practical essentials

For a substantial grocery purchase, a larger supermarket is usually easier.

Intermarché Hyper Menton on Avenue Saint-Roman offers a broader stock-up option, including online ordering and delivery services. Smaller central supermarkets are useful for replacing milk, coffee, fruit and whatever you've forgotten.

If you're carrying several bags on foot, check your return route before making a large purchase.

For apartment guests without a car, two smaller shopping trips may be more comfortable than one enormous one.

Our [supermarket guide for Menton](https://azurmenton.com/en/guide/supermarkets-in-menton) covers local options in more detail.

### A practical weekly shopping rhythm

| When | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| Sunday or Monday | Plan meals and check remaining supplies |
| Monday or Tuesday | Main grocery shop for 4–5 days |
| Wednesday | Visit the market for fresh produce |
| Friday | Buy ingredients for weekend meals and picnics |
| Throughout the week | Pick up bread, fruit and essentials as needed |

This is a suggested routine, not a strict timetable.

The idea is to reduce repetitive shopping while keeping enough flexibility to enjoy restaurants and spontaneous outings.

## 4. How much does a week of apartment living cost?

Accommodation is the biggest variable in a longer stay, so we've deliberately excluded it from the following calculations.

These are editorial spending allowances for groceries, dining out, transport and activities, not a survey of local prices. They exclude accommodation, travel to Menton, insurance and any refundable deposit. Laundry and other extras need their own allowance.

### Weekly budget per adult, excluding accommodation

| Expense | Economical | Comfortable | Experience-focused |

| Groceries and household consumables | €75 | €100 | €125 |
| Restaurants, cafés and drinks | €90 | €205 | €340 |
| Local transport and excursions | €20 | €45 | €75 |
| Activities and entertainment | €25 | €70 | €160 |
| Weekly total | €210 | €420 | €700 |

These figures are planning scenarios, not recorded averages of actual visitor spending.

The economical budget assumes substantial home cooking, casual dining and mostly free activities.

The comfortable budget allows regular restaurant meals, café visits, public transport and paid excursions.

The experience-focused budget makes room for better restaurants, beach clubs and more frequent activities while still taking advantage of having a kitchen.

### Total spending for two, three or four weeks

| Stay length | Economical | Comfortable | Experience-focused |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 2 weeks | €420 | €840 | €1,400 |
| 3 weeks | €630 | €1,260 | €2,100 |

All totals are per adult. For two adults, double the baseline as a simple planning estimate, although shared groceries and household supplies may reduce the combined cost.

### Why can longer stays cost less per day?

A short holiday encourages spending.

You arrive, visit attractions, eat out frequently and try to fit several experiences into a limited number of days.

During a longer stay, that urgency disappears.

You can make breakfast, enjoy a free beach day, cook dinner occasionally and spread larger excursions across several weeks.

The comfortable weekly estimate above works out to €60 per person per day, compared with the €85 daily scenario used in our shorter-holiday budget guide.

The difference comes from a different pattern of behaviour, not necessarily cheaper prices.

It assumes more home cooking, fewer paid activities and a slower travel rhythm.

For a safer allowance, add around 10–15% for unexpected expenses or particularly active weeks.

## 5. What should you actually cook?

Nobody comes to Menton to spend every evening washing pans.

The goal isn't to recreate your entire domestic routine. It's to make eating simple enough that restaurants remain a pleasure rather than an obligation.

Here is one example of a realistic apartment week.

| Day | Lunch | Dinner |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Monday | Bakery sandwich and fruit | Pasta with tomatoes, pesto and salad |
| Tuesday | Lunch at a local restaurant | Cheese, bread, olives and vegetables |
| Wednesday | Leftovers or market picnic | Fish or vegetables with potatoes |
| Thursday | Lunch during an excursion | Simple apartment dinner |
| Friday | Salad or takeaway | Restaurant dinner |
| Saturday | Beach picnic | Homemade pasta or takeaway pizza |
| Sunday | Long restaurant lunch | Light dinner using remaining groceries |

This keeps food preparation manageable.

A useful approach is to prepare ingredients rather than elaborate dishes. Wash vegetables, keep cheese and fruit available, and plan meals that take no more than twenty minutes.

It also helps reduce food waste, especially towards the end of your stay.

## 6. Laundry: the small detail that changes everything

For a two-week stay, laundry is convenient.

For four weeks, it's practically essential.

The best solution is an apartment with a washing machine, but that isn't always available.

Check the property description before booking and ask whether detergent, a drying rack or a dryer is provided.

### If your apartment has a washing machine

Plan one or two laundry sessions each week.

Choose a day when you'll spend several hours at home rather than starting a wash just before leaving for Monaco.

In summer, lightweight clothing may dry fairly quickly in suitable conditions. However, avoid assuming that every balcony allows clothes drying, and follow the building's rules.

### If your apartment doesn't have a washing machine

Look for a local laverie automatique, the French term for a self-service laundrette.

One practical option to investigate is the laundry service listed at Intermarché Hyper Menton, Avenue Saint-Roman. Confirm machine availability, access and payment arrangements before relying on it.

As a rough allowance, set aside €8–15 for a wash-and-dry session, depending on machine size, detergent and drying time. This is an estimate, not a confirmed Menton laundrette tariff.

### How much clothing should you pack?

For a stay with convenient laundry facilities, seven to ten days of everyday clothes will often be enough.

Bring a few versatile pieces, comfortable shoes and suitable layers for the season.

You don't need four weeks of separate outfits.

## 7. Working remotely without losing the holiday

A longer stay may include working days.

That doesn't mean you need a coworking membership or a complicated digital nomad routine.

For many visitors, the apartment itself is the most practical place to work.

What matters is making work compatible with the rhythm of Menton.

### A realistic working day

7:30–9:00 a.m. Breakfast, coffee and a walk beside the sea.

9:00 a.m.–12:30 p.m. Focused work in the apartment.

12:30–2:00 p.m. Lunch, a few errands or a short walk.

2:00–5:00 p.m. Meetings or the remaining work session.

After 5:00 p.m. Swimming, a restaurant, a walk through the old town or a quiet evening.

Naturally, your schedule may differ if your clients or colleagues operate in another time zone.

The important thing is to give work a defined place in the day.

Otherwise, you risk spending your holiday half-working and half-feeling guilty about not exploring.

### Check these before booking

Ask about the internet connection's reliability and, where possible, speed.

A phone hotspot or mobile data plan is useful as a backup, but coverage and roaming allowances vary. Confirm your provider's conditions, particularly if you rely on a non-EU mobile contract.

Check whether there's a proper table, practical seating, power outlets and a quiet space for calls.

In summer, make sure the room can remain comfortable during the hottest hours.

Also consider the apartment's layout if two people need to work simultaneously.

A quiet apartment with a reliable connection is generally more valuable than a list of nearby coworking spaces.

## 8. Alternate local days and excursions

This is probably the single most useful principle for a longer stay in Menton:

Don't turn every day into a sightseeing day.

One of Menton's advantages is its location. You can visit other Riviera destinations and still return to the same apartment in the evening.

But travelling somewhere new every day becomes tiring, expensive and strangely repetitive.

Instead, alternate three types of days.

### Local days

Stay in Menton.

Visit the market, swim, read, work, cook, walk through the old town or discover a different section of the seafront.

These days don't require much planning.

### Excursion days

Choose one destination and enjoy it without rushing.

Monaco works well for museums, gardens and sightseeing.

Nice offers a larger city atmosphere, markets, galleries and museums.

Ventimiglia provides an easy introduction to the Italian side of the border, with food, markets and a different rhythm of life.

### Recovery days

Do less.

Sleep a little longer, take care of laundry, stock the fridge, sit beside the sea or enjoy an afternoon without obligations.

Recovery days aren't wasted holiday time.

They're often what makes a three-week trip more enjoyable than an exhausting seven-day itinerary.

| Day | Main plan | Evening |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Monday | Groceries, work or relaxed local day | Dinner in the apartment |
| Tuesday | Beach, gardens or old-town exploration | Casual restaurant |
| Wednesday | Day trip to Monaco or Italy | Light dinner at home |
| Thursday | Work, laundry and errands | Seafront walk |
| Friday | Menton beach and market | Dinner out |
| Saturday | Full-day excursion or cultural activity | Flexible |
| Sunday | Slow breakfast, rest and planning | Home-cooked meal or takeaway |

For a remote worker, move the excursions to free days and keep work sessions consistent.

For a fully retired or holiday-focused traveller, use the same framework more loosely.

There is no need to fill every empty space.

## 10. A two-week, three-week and four-week plan

### Two weeks: balance discovery with downtime

During the first week, focus on Menton.

Explore the town, identify your favourite cafés, visit the market and become familiar with the beaches and local transport.

Include one substantial excursion.

During the second week, add two or three trips farther along the coast, alternating them with restful days in Menton.

Suggested rhythm: Three to four excursions across fourteen days.

### Three weeks: develop a routine

The first week is about settling in.

The second can include more ambitious outings, cultural visits and exploration of nearby villages.

By the third week, you'll probably have found places you want to revisit.

You may also discover that spending an entire day doing almost nothing feels perfectly natural.

Suggested rhythm: Four to six excursions across twenty-one days.

### Four weeks: live at your own pace

A month allows a genuinely different experience.

You can repeat favourite activities, experiment with local cooking, schedule regular working days and explore destinations beyond the most obvious tourist stops.

There is no need to complete a list.

You can spend several days in Menton, take an excursion, then return to your ordinary seaside routine.

Suggested rhythm: Five to eight excursions across twenty-eight days, depending on energy, season and working commitments.

These are editorial suggestions, not sightseeing targets.

## 11. Public transport for longer stays

For most visitors staying centrally, a car isn't essential.

Menton has regional train connections along the coast, and the Zest bus network serves the town and surrounding area.

For longer stays, travel passes are worth comparing.

### Published adult transport prices checked on 5 November 2026

| Ticket or pass | Published adult price |
| --- | --- |
| Zest single ticket | €2 |
| Zest one-day pass | €5 |
| Zest seven-day pass | €13 |
| Zest monthly pass | €38 |
| Pass Sud Azur tourist, 7 days | €50 |
| Pass Sud Azur tourist, 14 days | €80 |

The local Zest monthly pass is based on the calendar month, not a rolling thirty-day period. It requires a Zest smartcard; the operator sells it from the 20th of the previous month to the 19th of the current month. A stay crossing two calendar months needs a fresh comparison with seven-day tickets.

Pass SudAzur Explore covers participating train, bus and tram networks in the Alpes-Maritimes and Monaco. The published coastal rail boundary is Menton Garavan, not Ventimiglia: do not assume the Italian section is included. Check the specific service, ticket medium and extra charges before buying. At Lignes d'Azur machines the operator states a refundable €2 deposit for the anonymous card.

Don't automatically buy a pass for the full length of your stay.

If you're spending most of your time walking around Menton, individual tickets may be more economical.

If you've planned multiple regional excursions within a particular week, a suitable travel pass may offer better value.

For train services, check [SNCF Connect](https://www.sncf-connect.com/) and compare actual fares for your travel dates and service updates before each excursion.

## 12. The little things people forget about apartment stays

Some problems only become noticeable after several days.

It's worth dealing with them early.

### Household supplies

Check whether the apartment provides toilet paper, washing-up liquid, laundry detergent, rubbish bags and basic cleaning products.

A starter supply doesn't necessarily mean the property provides unlimited replenishment for a month.

### Rubbish and recycling

Ask where and when to dispose of household waste, and how recycling works in the building.

Don't leave bags in corridors or common areas.

### Cleaning

For a longer stay, ask what cleaning is included, whether fresh linen or towels are provided during the visit, and whether optional cleaning services are available.

Some apartments operate on a self-maintenance basis between arrival and departure.

### Deliveries and parcels

If you're ordering groceries or receiving packages, confirm the correct delivery address and whether the building has an intercom.

Don't assume a holiday rental can receive mail or parcels on your behalf without prior agreement.

### Medicines and emergencies

Locate a nearby pharmacy during the first few days.

Bring enough of any regular medication for the full stay, accounting for possible travel delays, and check the rules for bringing it into France.

For serious emergencies in France, call 112.

### Seasonal differences

During summer, prioritise cooling, shade and early outdoor activities.

In winter, confirm heating, daylight-sensitive plans and seasonal opening hours.

Autumn can bring heavy rain, so keep excursion dates flexible.

For seasonal planning, explore [Menton in autumn](https://azurmenton.com/en/guide/menton-in-autumn) and our [other local guides](https://azurmenton.com/en/guide).

## 13. How to keep a month-long stay enjoyable

A longer holiday doesn't become better just because you visit more places.

Often, the opposite is true.

If you plan a different excursion every morning, book restaurants every evening and fill every gap with a must-see attraction, you'll recreate the pressure of a short holiday over a much longer period.

Try maintaining a few simple routines instead.

Find a morning walk you genuinely enjoy.

Keep your fridge stocked with easy breakfast ingredients.

Choose two or three restaurants you might like to revisit.

Set aside time each week for groceries, laundry and any work commitments.

Have at least one day without a fixed plan.

And leave room to change your mind.

A sunny morning might turn into an unexpected beach day. A rainy afternoon might be better spent cooking, reading or visiting a museum.

That's part of the appeal of having more time.

## 14. Frequently asked questions

### Is Menton a good place to stay for a month?

Yes, particularly if you enjoy a walkable coastal town with markets, restaurants, beaches and transport connections to nearby destinations. The experience is very different from a short sightseeing visit.

### Can I stay in Menton for several weeks without renting a car?

Yes. A central apartment, regional trains and local buses can cover many everyday needs and coastal excursions. A car may be useful for specific destinations but isn't essential for every visitor.

### How much spending money do I need for three weeks in Menton?

Excluding accommodation, a comfortable planning allowance is approximately €1,260 per adult for three weeks. More economical stays could work around €630, while experience-focused holidays may reach €2,100 or more. Actual spending depends heavily on dining and activities.

### Is it cheaper to cook in an apartment?

Usually, cooking a portion of your meals reduces costs compared with eating out every day. The saving depends on how you shop, what you cook and how much food you waste.

### How many excursions should I plan for a two-week stay?

Three or four substantial day trips are a reasonable starting point. Add more if you enjoy frequent travel, or fewer if you prefer beaches and slow days.

### Should I book an apartment with a washing machine?

It's highly convenient, particularly for three or four weeks. If there's no washing machine, check for accessible local laundry facilities before arrival.

### Can I work remotely from an apartment in Menton?

Potentially, yes, provided the internet connection and workspace suit your needs. Confirm the practical details with the property before booking, and check any employment, immigration or insurance conditions that apply to your circumstances.

## Final thoughts: make Menton your temporary home

A weekend in Menton is about discovering the town.

A week is about enjoying it.

But two, three or four weeks give you time to build a small life around the Mediterranean.

You'll learn where to buy your favourite bread. You'll find the best time to visit the market. You'll probably start recognising familiar faces along your morning walk.

You'll also discover that a perfectly ordinary day can be one of the most memorable parts of a longer holiday.

A little planning makes all of that easier.

Choose the right apartment, organise a manageable grocery and laundry routine, keep your weekly spending under control and leave enough space between excursions.

Then allow yourself to enjoy Menton without treating every day like a checklist.
