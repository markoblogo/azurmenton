# What to Do in Menton on Sundays and Public Holidays

You've arrived in Menton on Saturday evening. The Mediterranean looks beautiful, the apartment is comfortable and you're ready to start exploring.

Then you remember: tomorrow is Sunday.

Will the supermarket be open? Can you buy fresh bread? Is it worth planning a restaurant dinner? And what happens if Monday is a French public holiday?

Fortunately, Menton doesn't simply shut down every Sunday. It's a living town and a tourist destination, with markets, restaurants, cafés and plenty of things to enjoy outdoors.

But opening hours are less predictable than on an ordinary weekday.

Some supermarkets close at lunchtime. Independent businesses follow different weekly schedules. Museums may have their own closing days, and public holidays can change arrangements that normally work perfectly well on Sundays.

The solution isn't to memorise a list of businesses supposedly open all year.

It's to plan the essentials in advance, verify the places that matter and build a day that doesn't depend on everything being open.

Here's how.

The crucial point is that Sunday opening doesn't guarantee public-holiday opening.

A shop that normally opens every Sunday may close on Christmas Day or 1 May.

Similarly, a restaurant that serves Sunday lunch may not open for dinner.

## 2. The easiest solution: organise Saturday properly

If you're staying in an apartment, a little preparation on Saturday makes Sunday much more relaxed.

You don't need an enormous grocery shop.

Just make sure you're not depending on a particular business being open when you're hungry.

### A practical Saturday shopping list

- Bread or breakfast pastries for the following morning, if suitable for storage.

- Coffee, tea, milk and breakfast ingredients.

- Fruit, yoghurt, eggs or another simple breakfast.

- Cheese, tomatoes, olives and something for lunch.

- Ingredients for one easy dinner.

- Drinking water if you prefer bottled water.

- Snacks for children or excursions.

- Any necessary medication or personal essentials.

For a couple, a simple apartment meal could be fresh pasta, salad, bread and fruit.

For families, stocking a few familiar foods can be particularly useful if your Sunday plans change unexpectedly.

If you intend to eat out, reserve the restaurant before Sunday rather than waiting until early evening.

Our advice: Have enough food in the apartment for breakfast and one substantial meal. That gives you flexibility without forcing you to cook throughout your holiday.

## 3. Buying groceries on Sunday in Menton

Menton has more Sunday shopping options than visitors sometimes expect.

But the type of shop matters.

### Les Halles: the traditional Sunday morning option

Address: Quai de Monléon, central Menton.

The municipal covered market is one of the town's most enjoyable places for fresh food.

According to the City of Menton, the market operates every day, including Sundays, from 7:30 a.m. to 1 p.m.

You'll find traders selling fruit, vegetables, cheese, meat and other food products.

It's an excellent place to buy ingredients for a Mediterranean lunch.

However, the published market schedule doesn't mean every stall will be present every day.

Holiday schedules and individual trader absences can affect what's available.

If you need one very specific ingredient, don't rely entirely on a single market visit.

Official information: [Municipal Halles market hours](https://www.menton.fr/Marche-des-Halles-Municipales.html).

## 4. What about French public holidays?

A public holiday isn't simply another Sunday.

French law distinguishes between different types of holidays, and individual businesses may close even when they're normally open on that weekday.

Most French public holidays don't automatically require every business to close.

But 1 May, Labour Day, has special legal rules and is generally a mandatory non-working day, with exceptions for activities that cannot be interrupted.

This makes 1 May particularly important for holiday planning.

### Public holidays worth checking carefully

| Holiday | Typical date | Planning advice |
| --- | --- | --- |
| New Year's Day | 1 January | Arrange food and restaurant plans beforehand |
| Easter Monday | Varies | Expect changes to normal Monday schedules |
| Labour Day | 1 May | Plan for substantial closures |
| Victory in Europe Day | 8 May | Check shops, attractions and transport |
| Ascension Day | Varies | Popular long-weekend travel period |
| Whit Monday | Varies | Confirm individual opening hours |
| Bastille Day | 14 July | Expect celebrations, crowds and possible traffic changes |
| Assumption Day | 15 August | Busy summer holiday; reserve restaurants |
| All Saints' Day | 1 November | Verify shopping and attraction hours |
| Armistice Day | 11 November | Check reduced services and opening |
| Christmas Day | 25 December | Plan meals and essential shopping well in advance |

Easter Monday, Ascension Day and Whit Monday move from year to year.

For exact dates, consult the [French government's official holiday calendar](https://www.service-public.gouv.fr/particuliers/vosdroits/F2405).

### The special case of 1 May

If you're visiting Menton on 1 May, organise your groceries and dinner beforehand.

Don't assume that a bakery or supermarket will open just because its ordinary schedule includes Sundays or holidays.

The same caution applies to activities requiring staff, although certain essential services can continue operating.

The sea and public promenades remain attractive alternatives, subject to weather and any temporary restrictions.

## 5. Restaurants on Sunday: lunch is not the same as dinner

Sunday dining can be very enjoyable in Menton.

It's a natural day for a long Mediterranean lunch, particularly along the waterfront.

But you shouldn't assume that every restaurant serving lunch will reopen in the evening.

Some establishments have one service only, while others close on Sunday evenings or operate seasonally.

### A reliable restaurant strategy

If you want a substantial meal, consider booking Sunday lunch.

A long lunch offers time to enjoy local food without needing a late dinner reservation.

If you prefer dinner, confirm the actual Sunday evening service before making plans.

Don't rely on a restaurant's general “open Sunday” label. Look for the specific meal period and available reservation times.

### What if you haven't booked?

Start with the restaurant's official website or booking platform.

Check whether it accepts walk-ins and whether the kitchen is operating at your intended time.

If you're visiting during the Lemon Festival, a summer weekend or a French holiday weekend, availability may be more limited.

For an apartment stay, a practical backup is to keep one easy meal at home.

It means that failing to find a restaurant doesn't have to ruin the evening.

## 6. The best things to do in Menton when shops are closed

One of Menton's great advantages is how much you can enjoy without buying a ticket or depending on business opening hours.

Here are several reliable ideas.

### Walk along Promenade du Soleil

Menton's seafront promenade is one of the simplest Sunday activities.

Enjoy views of the Mediterranean, Belle Époque buildings, palm trees and the changing light over the water.

The tourist office lists Promenade du Soleil as freely accessible throughout the year.

Cafés are an optional extra, not a requirement.

This makes it ideal for a Sunday morning or a public holiday when you don't want to depend on reservations.

### Explore Jardins Biovès

Near the tourist office, Jardins Biovès offers landscaped flower beds, palms, fountains and sculpture.

The tourist office lists the garden as open daily throughout the year, with free admission under normal conditions.

During the Fête du Citron, however, special event arrangements may restrict or ticket certain areas.

Outside major events, it's a straightforward short walk.

### Enjoy Esplanade des Sablettes

This is one of the best places to admire Menton's colourful old town from below.

The esplanade offers panoramic views of the buildings, basilica and Mediterranean.

The public space is listed as freely accessible year-round.

In good weather, you can spend an entire afternoon here without entering a museum, booking a tour or buying anything.

Some beach services and commercial facilities are seasonal, but the public waterfront is the main attraction.

### Explore the old-town streets

Menton's old town is beautiful on a quiet Sunday.

Walk through the lower pedestrian streets, admire the colourful buildings and explore the lanes climbing towards Saint-Michel Basilica.

The streets themselves don't depend on shop opening hours.

However, entry into churches, museums and other buildings may be affected by services, ceremonies or published schedules.

Be prepared for steep slopes and steps in the upper old town.

### Pack a picnic

A picnic may be the most dependable Sunday lunch in Menton.

Buy your ingredients on Saturday or at the morning market.

Bring bread, cheese, tomatoes, fruit and water.

Enjoy them in a suitable public picnic area or another location where eating is permitted.

Respect local rules, avoid leaving litter and don't obstruct pathways.

A picnic is particularly useful when travelling with children or when your day's timing is uncertain.

## 8. Museums, gardens and attractions: don't assume Sunday closure

Sunday can actually be a good day to visit certain attractions.

For example, Val Rahmeh Botanical Garden normally opens on Sundays and closes on Tuesdays.

That illustrates why a generic rule such as “museums close on Sundays” doesn't work.

However, official seasonal hours and temporary closures take priority over the ordinary weekly schedule.

Val Rahmeh is closed from 2 through 27 November 2026 inclusive for works, according to the operator. That closure overrides the ordinary Sunday schedule, including 8, 15 and 22 November. Do not substitute another paid garden without checking its own season.

Before visiting a paid attraction, check three things:

The regular weekly schedule. Does the venue usually open on Sunday?

The seasonal calendar. Are winter hours or annual closures in effect?

The exact date. Is there a special public-holiday closure, private event or maintenance interruption?

If the visit requires advance booking, confirm an actual available session.

For an attraction-dependent itinerary, always have a free outdoor alternative.

## 9. Public transport on Sundays and holidays

Menton's location makes trips to Monaco, Nice and Ventimiglia tempting, even on Sundays.

But don't assume that weekday transport schedules apply.

### Regional trains

Check your actual journey through [SNCF Connect](https://www.sncf-connect.com/) or the relevant regional train operator.

Look at the outbound and return journeys.

A destination may be easy to reach in the morning but less convenient to return from late in the evening.

Weekend engineering works, public-holiday timetables or temporary disruptions can also affect service. Checked 5 November 2026: TER Sud's Nice–Ventimiglia works notice runs through 10 December 2027 and describes interruptions from 22:00 to 06:00 on nights Sunday–Thursday. Sunday evening is included. Check the dated journey planner, actual last return and replacement arrangements; an ordinary daytime timetable is not a late-night guarantee.

For trips into Italy, verify the operating company and international service conditions.

### Local buses

The Zest network serves Menton and nearby communities.

Use the official [Zest website](https://www.zestbus.fr/) to check Sunday and holiday service rather than relying on a weekday timetable.

Some routes may operate less frequently, and certain services may not run.

### Driving

If you're planning to drive, check road restrictions and event closures.

This is particularly important during the Lemon Festival, major Riviera events and Menton's pedestrian Sundays.

Parking access may also change during local festivities.

Our advice: For a holiday Sunday, choose one main excursion rather than constructing an itinerary with multiple tight transport connections.

## 10. What if it rains on Sunday?

Rain doesn't automatically ruin a day in Menton, but it makes opening hours more important.

An outdoor walk is easy to organise spontaneously.

A museum, workshop or indoor attraction requires a little more verification.

### Reliable rainy-day planning

Check which museums or cultural venues are genuinely open on that date.

Look for cinema screenings or indoor activities with published Sunday sessions.

Consider a long lunch if you've confirmed restaurant availability.

If you're staying in an apartment, a relaxed afternoon with food from the market, a book or a film can also be pleasant.

For families, keeping an indoor activity or simple entertainment at the apartment can be especially helpful.

Don't assume that the tourist office, a workshop or every museum will be open simply because it's raining and you need somewhere to go.

## 11. What about pharmacies and other essentials?

Not every Sunday problem concerns sightseeing.

You might need medicine, baby supplies or something you've forgotten to buy.

### Pharmacies

French pharmacies operate a duty system for periods outside normal opening hours.

The pharmacy on duty may change depending on the date, time and area.

Don't rely on a permanently published list of “Sunday pharmacies.”

Check the local duty pharmacy information for your specific date or contact a nearby pharmacy for instructions.

Some duty services operate behind closed doors and require a particular access procedure.

For a medical emergency, call 112. This is access information, not medical advice; do not delay urgent care while searching for a shop or duty rota.

### Baby supplies and household essentials

If you're travelling with a baby, buy enough nappies, formula and other essential supplies before a major holiday.

The same applies to specific dietary products, pet food and other items that may not be stocked by small convenience shops.

Even when a supermarket is open, it may not carry your preferred brand.

### Apartment arrival on a Sunday

If you're checking into an apartment on Sunday, confirm the check-in instructions, access code or key collection arrangements in advance.

Ask whether the property provides initial supplies such as toilet paper, washing-up liquid and coffee.

That makes the first evening easier, particularly if you arrive after local supermarkets have closed.

## 12. How to check opening hours properly

Online opening hours are useful, but they aren't always current.

For a normal weekday, a map listing may be sufficient for casual plans.

For Sunday dinner or Christmas Day, use a more reliable process.

### Step 1: Start with the business's own website

Look for the official opening schedule and any holiday notice.

A store chain's branch page is more useful than the chain's general homepage.

### Step 2: Check the exact date

A business may list normal Sunday hours but have a separate exception for 1 November, 11 November or Christmas.

Look for terms such as horaires exceptionnels or jours fériés.

### Step 3: Check reservations or booking availability

For restaurants and ticketed attractions, an active booking system may give more useful information than general opening hours.

However, a page saying “open” doesn't guarantee that there are tables or tickets available.

### Step 4: Call when it really matters

If you're travelling a long distance, relying on accessible facilities or arranging an important meal, telephone confirmation is sensible.

Here's a useful French question:

“Bonjour, êtes-vous ouverts dimanche prochain ? Et est-ce que vous servez le dîner ?”

Meaning: “Hello, are you open next Sunday? And do you serve dinner?”

For public holidays, replace dimanche prochain with the specific date.

### Step 5: Keep a backup

If your first-choice restaurant is closed, you should already know whether you'll cook, order takeaway from a confirmed provider or visit another restaurant.

For attractions, keep a coastal walk or garden visit as your alternative.

The best Sunday itinerary isn't the one with the most places supposedly open.

It's the one that still works when one of them isn't.

## 13. Three easy Sunday plans

### Plan A: The relaxed Mediterranean Sunday

Start with coffee and breakfast at your apartment or a confirmed open bakery.

Visit Les Halles during its morning trading period.

Walk through the lower town towards the seafront.

Enjoy a long lunch at a restaurant you've reserved.

Spend the afternoon on Promenade du Soleil or at Les Sablettes.

Return to the apartment for a light dinner.

Why it works: Only the market and restaurant depend on business hours. The rest of the day is flexible.

### Plan B: A Sunday with almost no opening-hour dependencies

Prepare breakfast and a picnic from groceries purchased the previous day.

Walk through Jardins Biovès and towards the waterfront.

Explore Promenade du Soleil and the lower pedestrian streets.

Enjoy lunch outdoors in an appropriate location.

Spend the afternoon admiring the old town from Esplanade des Sablettes.

Cook dinner in your apartment.

Why it works: The plan depends primarily on public spaces and weather rather than commercial opening hours.

### Plan C: A Sunday excursion

Choose Monaco, Nice or Ventimiglia.

Check the outbound and return train services.

Plan one main activity and verify its Sunday opening.

Book lunch if you want a particular restaurant.

Return to Menton without making your evening dependent on a late connection.

Why it works: It avoids the common mistake of scheduling too many destinations around reduced transport services.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is everything closed in Menton on Sunday?

No. The covered market normally operates on Sunday mornings, some supermarkets open and restaurants may serve lunch or dinner. Individual businesses follow their own schedules.

### Is Menton's covered market open on Sunday?

The municipality publishes daily opening from 7:30 a.m. to 1 p.m., including Sundays. Individual stall availability and holiday exceptions may vary.

### Can I buy groceries on Sunday evening in Menton?

Some convenience supermarkets publish evening Sunday hours, including certain Carrefour City branches. Check the exact branch and any exceptional holiday schedule before travelling.

### Are restaurants open on Sunday night?

Some are, but not all. A restaurant may serve Sunday lunch and close in the evening. Confirm dinner service and reserve when appropriate.

### What can I do in Menton if shops are closed?

Explore Promenade du Soleil, Jardins Biovès, the lower town and Esplanade des Sablettes. These public spaces don't normally depend on commercial opening hours, although weather and special events can affect access.

### Is 1 May different from other French public holidays?

Yes. Labour Day has special legal protections as a non-working day, so visitors should prepare for wider closures and organise essential purchases beforehand.

### Do trains run on Sundays?

Regional trains operate on Sundays, but timetables and frequencies can differ. Check the actual journey, including your return, before departure.

### Is Menton a good destination for a Sunday visit?

Yes. Its waterfront, gardens and historic streets make it rewarding even without a full programme of shops and attractions.

## Final thoughts: let Sunday be Sunday

Sunday in Menton doesn't need to be complicated.

You might find yourself shopping at the market, enjoying a long lunch or discovering a quieter section of the seafront.

On a public holiday, you may encounter more closed doors than usual.

But the Mediterranean will still be there.

The best approach is to plan the practical things early, confirm the few businesses that matter and leave the rest of the day open.

Buy your breakfast ingredients, organise dinner, check your transport and choose a walk you can enjoy without an admission ticket.

Then relax.

A slow Sunday is part of the Riviera experience, not a problem that needs solving.
